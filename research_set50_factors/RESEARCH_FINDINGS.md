## Stability, regimes, and turnover

One-day reversal is a short-horizon effect: evaluation Rank IC falls from **0.0477** at one session to about **−0.0009** at ten and **0.0030** at twenty. Five-session reversal Rank IC is **0.0237**; among 24 non-overlapping evaluation segments, **19** have positive mean Rank IC, with five negative segments. Its five-session Rank IC is higher in the historical negative benchmark-momentum group (**0.0352**, 197 dates) than in the positive group (**0.0154**, 277 dates), and higher in the high-volatility group (**0.0327**, 309 dates) than the low-volatility group (**0.0067**, 165 dates). Those are exploratory differences, not established regime rules. The four predeclared interaction tests do not establish a stable incremental effect. `segment_ic.csv`, rolling plots, and regime tables preserve the unfavorable periods.

The historical top-five proxy uses adjusted opens only to measure desired turnover and a **commission-only** cost floor. For one-day reversal, mean one-way turnover per 20-session evaluation window is **34.38×**, **6.31×**, and **2.75×** initial capital with 1-, 5-, and 10-session rebalancing. Mean corresponding commission-plus-VAT floors are about **THB 577,458**, **THB 105,990**, and **THB 46,143** per THB 10 million window. A separate 1%-of-prior-volume cap, 100-share lots, sell-before-buy cash accounting, and unfilled-share records show substantial execution constraints. These are scenario quantities, not achieved fills or net strategy returns.

The 16-session raw-tick reference applies the actual price-dependent exchange ladder to observed raw first-tick prices. Across the selected-name references its median commission-plus-two-adverse-tick round-trip estimate is about **1.44%**. That is a price reference, not an actual fill model: order-book depth, IOC behavior, partial fills, and the longer historical period cannot be verified. No tick-size calculation is applied to adjusted historical opens.

## Phase 1 interpretation and Phase 3 decision

Phase 1 momentum's native losses cannot be attributed solely to costs: its tested short-term continuation ranking is itself negative at a one-session target. Longer momentum signals remain statistically unstable, so this does not explain every holding period. Phase 1 equal-weight relative strength reproduced momentum exactly, matching the algebra. Inverse-volatility sizing worsened that native return comparison while changing turnover and risk; Phase 2 does not isolate a general sizing effect. RSI's +1.2358 percentage-point native excess occurred with only 34.2% average exposure, negative payoff, and no supporting five-session oversold ranking evidence. Cash participation is a plausible explanation, not a proven attribution.

Phase 3 should compare a **one-day reversal tie-breaker** against otherwise identical planned trades, measuring its incremental turnover and actual fills. It should separately examine risk-based sizing and exposure/drawdown management without calling volatility an alpha factor. The 20-session competition needs completed FIFO round-trips, average exposure above 50%, positive expected payoff, drawdown control, and nonnegative absolute and excess return. Phase 2 statistics do not establish any of those. No current factor is proven profitable or ready for official eligibility claims.
